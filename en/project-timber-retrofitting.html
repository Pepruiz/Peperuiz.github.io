Case Study · Structural Retrofitting & Rehabilitation

Historic Building Retrofitting in Central Madrid

Comprehensive structural intervention and reinforcement of an 1862 timber-framed residential building

Original Year 1862
Location Madrid City Centre
Intervention Comprehensive Retrofit
Role Technical Direction & Analysis

Origins and Baseline Condition

During the historical and documentary research phase prior to project drafting, technical investigation at Madrid's Municipal Archives (Archivo de la Villa) accurately dated the building to 1862, correcting official cadastral records that erroneously dated it to 1900. The property represents the quintessential mid-19th-century construction typology of Madrid's historic core: perimeter load-bearing masonry walls, direct rubble-stone foundations, and traditional timber frames composed of vertical posts (pies derechos) and horizontal beams (carreras).

The building's structural trajectory was marked by an emergency municipal intervention in 2000. Due to imminent collapse hazards, a partial emergency demolition took place that left roughly 25% of the floor plan reduced to a bare skeleton: solely timber posts and beams stripped of wall infills. This critical condition, with exhausted safety margins, turned the project into a high-stakes structural challenge requiring complete stabilization and structural reconstruction to meet modern Eurocode and Structural Code standards.

Foundations: Ground Stabilization and Underpinning

Intervention at the foundation level was critical due to the extreme heterogeneity of the original footings and severe degradation of lime mortars within the masonry. Structural diagnostic testing identified zones where masonry thickness was insufficient to sustain renewed design loads, requiring a tailored hybrid underpinning scheme.

To address deficient bearing capacity, a curtain of 180 mm micropiles was installed in strategic sectors. This solution not only underpinned existing perimeter walls but also provided earth retention and ground stabilization adjacent to neighbouring historic buildings, preventing differential settlements within a dense urban context.

Complementarily, a reinforced concrete basement raft foundation was engineered with dual functionality: acting as a ground-level rigid tying system unifying all load-bearing masonry walls while simultaneously serving as a renewed bearing surface. This system guarantees that the entire building behaves as a solidary unit against potential ground movements.

Vertical Structure Consolidation

The technical strategy prioritised life safety through structural interventions that respect historic character while eliminating catastrophic failure risks. For the original cast-iron hollow columns, their historic morphology was preserved intact while their axial load capacity was upgraded by filling their hollow core with high-performance non-shrink grout. As a result, the columns now sustain substantially higher axial loads without external jacketing or visual alteration.

Regarding timber posts and lintels, steel structural sections were integrated to form a collaborative composite framework, ensuring safe load transfer down to the foundation. Furthermore, in areas where emergency demolitions occurred in 2000, masonry infill walls were systematically reconstructed with brickwork, re-establishing original framing and restoring global three-dimensional load distribution throughout the property.

Floor Slabs: Timber-Concrete Composite Systems

In the floor systems, the primary objective was conserving every timber joist exhibiting adequate structural capacity. Where timber displayed biological decay or mechanical degradation, joists were reinforced or substituted with rolled IPE steel profiles. Once the joist grid was stabilized, ribbed steel decking (nervometal) was installed as lost formwork for the new compression topping.

To achieve composite monolithic action, mechanical shear connectors were fixed into both timber and steel joists. The inclusion of welded wire fabric within the concrete compression slab produced a high-rigidity timber-concrete composite diaphragm. This solution not only elevates vertical load resistance but transforms each floor into a rigid horizontal diaphragm that braces all perimeter walls against lateral wind and seismic actions.

Conclusion

The rehabilitation of this Madrid historic property exemplifies how an endangered building slated for condemnation can be technically recovered. What began as an emergency demolition evolved into a precision structural intervention that went beyond raw reinforcement to deeply understand the mechanics of an 1862 timber-framed construction. Through the synergy of preserved historic timber, auxiliary steel members, and composite slabs, structural safety has been reinstated while safeguarding architectural heritage.

The project's success lies in applying tailored structural engineering at each vulnerable node: from micropile underpinning that safeguarded adjacent party walls to internal column grouting that magnified axial resistance invisibly. The result is the transformation of a precarious structure into a fully consolidated, code-compliant, and durable building for future generations.
