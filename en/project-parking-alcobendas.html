Case Study · Infrastructure & Public Works

Semi-Underground Parking Facility

Structural engineering and infrastructure for an urban mobility hub topped with an accessible public park

Client Local Public Authority
Location Community of Madrid
Year 2025
Role Structural Redesign & Analysis

Geotechnical Context, Topography, and Rooftop Surcharges

Commissioned by a municipal authority within the Madrid metropolitan area, this project comprised the engineering of a semi-underground two-storey parking facility. A primary structural challenge was the asymmetric topography: surrounding hillside grades imposed severe lateral earth pressure on one longitudinal facade, requiring a double-height reinforced concrete earth-retaining wall, while the opposite longitudinal facade remained open to facilitate natural daylighting and cross-ventilation.

In addition to asymmetrical geotechnical loads, the upper roof level was conceived as an accessible urban landscaped park. This architectural requirement introduced substantial dead loads, including up to 60 cm of dense soil substrate for mature vegetation, heavy concrete street furniture, and occasional maintenance and emergency vehicle wheel loads, translating into an exceptionally severe permanent surcharge over the roof slab.

Original Precast Concrete Scheme

In the original construction project designed by a prior engineering firm, the roof was specified as a one-way precast system using hollow-core slabs and prestressed concrete planks spanning between an orthogonal grid of cast-in-place concrete beams and columns. The initial intent was to expedite execution schedules through large-format prefabricated components.

During initial assembly, precast hollow-core units were hoisted and seated onto beam supports, with top negative reinforcement and welded wire distribution meshes arranged in preparation for the cast-in-place compression topping pour.

Structural Pathology, Progressive Sagging, and Collapse

A severe structural failure occurred during the concreting of the upper level's compression topping. Latent manufacturing defects in the pretensioned prestressing strands—specifically a critical failure of bond adhesion and anchorage—caused an abrupt loss of load capacity under the dead load of fresh concrete.

The failure triggered the catastrophic collapse of an entire structural bay, which crashed into the lower level. Immediate forensic investigation revealed that adjacent unbroken bays were experiencing progressive, irreversible sagging, with uncontrolled deflections and the complete loss of prestress camber.

Forensic engineering reports concluded that the entire production batch of precast units suffered from latent defects that invalidated normative structural safety factors. Facing an unacceptable risk of progressive collapse and the impossibility of certifying service life, the municipal authority ordered the complete demolition of all erected superstructure to restart with an uncompromisingly safe design.

Technical Redesign: In-Situ Waffle Slab and Peikko Punching Shear Reinforcement

Following debris removal, RC Estructuras was entrusted with the comprehensive re-engineering and typological reformulation of the facility. The precast approach was fundamentally rejected in favor of an in-situ two-way reinforced concrete waffle slab with recoverable void formers.

This design provided decisive structural benefits: 3D indeterminate load redistribution, enhanced ductility, monolithic joint continuity, and an optimized overall structural depth that increased clear interior vehicle headroom while maintaining existing park surface finished grades.

The most demanding analytical verification was punching shear resistance at column-slab connections on the second floor, due to high concentrated reactions from the heavy landscaped soil above. To resist these extreme shear stresses without dropping bulky capitals (which would compromise vehicle clearance) or enlarging column dimensions, Peikko PSB punching shear stud rails were engineered. These forged-head steel studs were placed radially within the solid column drop panels, mechanically anchoring internal diagonal concrete compression struts in strict accordance with Eurocode 2 and the Structural Code.

Conclusion

The semi-underground parking project exemplifies how rigorous structural engineering can recover a hazardous public construction crisis and turn it into a durable, resilient civic asset.

Substituting vulnerable precast systems with an in-situ two-way waffle slab reinforced with high-capacity Peikko shear studs guaranteed full structural reliability, effortlessly absorbed asymmetric lateral earth thrusts, and delivered a safe, vibrant green park to the local community.
